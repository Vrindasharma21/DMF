fault-lines of the fault in our stars

The hero of the book, seventeen year old blue eyed Augustus Waters who has one real and another prosthetic leg loves metaphors. He keeps wasting playing with unlit cigarettes by keeping them in his mouth and not lighting them - which symbolises some bullshit around you being surrounded with death and not letting it kill you. Sixteen year old Hazel Grace Lancaster who moves around with a cannula and oxygen tank is put on Phalanxiphor, a fictional medicine that has stopped her tumour growth and extended her death deadline. How does she choose to make use of this time ? By re-reading a drunkard author’s only book a million times - this book is the fucking base of her own individual life and her love life. Rightfully so, she will die sometime but not now. Gus, as the hero is lovingly called in the novel, is cancer free for the last 14 months and he will not die. Instead, he will befriend other dying cancer kids to charm them with his looks. He is blue eyed remember ? I so badly want to meet a blue eyed date-able man in real life. Anyways, this is the premise.

For the first half of the novel we have to feel bad for Hazel and then after a predictable plot twist, Gus relapses and now he will die. Thankfully, we have to feel bad for him only for a little while because he dies pretty quickly and then the final quarter of the book becomes about Hazel trying to find a love letter Gus left for her.
