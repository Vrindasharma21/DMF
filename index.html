The DRAMA — Reviewing the Guy’s Dilemma

It’s a romantic film with two very, very attractive people — of course I am sold.

The Drama — Plot

Emma (Zendaya) and Charlie (Robert) are truly in love with each other and preparing for their impending wedding. They are writing their wedding speeches, doing dance rehearsals, finalising the menu — all while looking spot-on and being cutely in love. This modern love loses its normality when Charlie, along with two close friends at a “finalising-the-menu dinner”, discovers that Emma had planned a mass shooting as a teenager. The remaining film explores how Charlie and Emma’s “relationship of love” responds to this twisted fact.
Before anyone else can react, the maid of honour (Rachel) makes her shock and contempt at this obviously disturbing piece of information very clear. She is enraged and disgusted by Emma because her cousin has been paralysed from another unrelated mass shooting that had actually happened. Her exaggerated reaction symbolises one side of society for me.
This also scares the shit out of Charlie for different, more natural reasons. He feels like he no longer knows who he is in love with — the sexy, funny, empathetic Emma or a funny-looking, unfunny teenage psychopath who plans mass shootings. Rachel’s husband (Mike) tries to bring Rachel back to her senses while Emma, moments after her confession, pukes on the dinner table. I would have called her reaction unreasonable had I not known someone who had puked his entire childhood when anxious. Emma was clearly anxious at being lashed out at. She was also scared and sad.
