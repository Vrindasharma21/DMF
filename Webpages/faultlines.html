fault-lines of the fault in our stars

The hero of the book, seventeen year old blue eyed Augustus Waters who has one real and another prosthetic leg loves metaphors. He keeps wasting playing with unlit cigarettes by keeping them in his mouth and not lighting them - which symbolises some bullshit around you being surrounded with death and not letting it kill you. Sixteen year old Hazel Grace Lancaster who moves around with a cannula and oxygen tank is put on Phalanxiphor, a fictional medicine that has stopped her tumour growth and extended her death deadline. How does she choose to make use of this time ? By re-reading a drunkard author’s only book a million times - this book is the fucking base of her own individual life and her love life. Rightfully so, she will die sometime but not now. Gus, as the hero is lovingly called in the novel, is cancer free for the last 14 months and he will not die. Instead, he will befriend other dying cancer kids to charm them with his looks. He is blue eyed remember ? I so badly want to meet a blue eyed date-able man in real life. Anyways, this is the premise.

For the first half of the novel we have to feel bad for Hazel and then after a predictable plot twist, Gus relapses and now he will die. Thankfully, we have to feel bad for him only for a little while because he dies pretty quickly and then the final quarter of the book becomes about Hazel trying to find a love letter Gus left for her. If Karan Johar were to write a book, it would be this. Let me support my case.

  • It is about two white American teenagers who do everything else other than study - from throwing eggs at a friend’s exes house (thats my favourite part of the novel though, Geet !!) to having sex in Amsterdam. Fuck off, you are not even 18 yet, forget being an IIT graduate consultant !!
  • Money is never a problem in their cancer treatment or life in general. As a part of Cancer perks they receive from benevolent NGOs they visit Amsterdam, drink starry champagne their, stay in a fancy hotel, eat passion fruit cremeux and dragon carrot risotto and much more, all fucking free of cost.
  • Lastly and most Bollywood-ly they kiss in the Anne Frank house and people around them start clapping. In a museum, overlapping with a video of Otto Frank talking about his dead family, two teenagers are making out and the visitors, enjoy that show and convey their approval with a cheer. Wow, fuck my life.

The book is not that bad through. I got a bit carried away by hate because I am enjoying hating on things off lately. Anyways! The lead upto Augustus’s death is beautifully written. Perspectives on life, death and illness have been written about in a palatable way. And howsoever unreal the Amsterdam sequence might have been, it got me dreaming, so yeah, the book is fancy but not foul. The only complaint that I have is that it treats its central love story too lightly. There are the usual spending days together, teasing each other, praising each other, undressing each other - but is that love ? Isn’t that the definition of sex + friendship. Romance must have something more to it. The eulogies that they have written for each other are a proof of the same - the love story isn’t very lovely.

Fault in our stars by John Green is a book I was accidentally gifted by a friend who was packing away his stuff from his hostel room and didn’t have “enough space”. I understand him now. It is like not having enough time to do a certain thing. Well, if you like the thing enough, you will find the time. Similarly, if he wanted to carry it, he would have. This book becomes extra baggage once you have read it. It won’t rally move you or anything, but it will pass your time nicely enough that you don’t regret reading it. Mid, period
