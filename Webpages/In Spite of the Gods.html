In Spite of the Gods

In spite of the Gods is a book written by a journalist Edward Luce published in 2007. I came across this book while I was blog-hopping. A certain centrist atheist sexist 40 year gentleman had appreciated it. Judging by its cover and title, it seemed impressive, and I ended up ordering it.

The book in no way wishes to talk about the cliche of India being the land of elephants and a philosophical retreat as most foreigners often view it. In fact, it aims to honour its pluralism, democracy and diversity and talk about the multitude of problems we face. The book is divided into 8 chapters. Frankly speaking, the first four chapters give an impression of it being no more than an NCERT civics textbook with the exception of interesting chapter titles. Take for example — “Many crescents” for a chapter on the diversity of Indian Muslims and “Hers to Lose” for a concluding chapter talking about how India is on the ascent and the major fuckups it has to pull and has been pulling to detract herself from development. My favourite one is “The Imaginary Horse” which talks about the militant Hindu nationalists and their attempts at rewriting imagined history.

However, I realised that the first half of the book seemed boring to me because most of what is written is a commonplace discussion today. Which, if we think about it, is a strength of the book indicating the correct judgement of its author. It only adds more to his credibility, but sadly to the reader’s redundancy. If only, I didn’t watch “Saat Bahuraniyaan” with my grandmother as a kid and had read this book, life would have been simpler. Anyways, as we progress, the contents of the book become increasingly interesting and important considering the status quo of Indian journalism.

It has never been as important as today, to talk about the condition of Indian Muslims, the intentions of Hindu nationalists and the corruption of our beloved bureaucracy. Every night, I read this, slept and my disgust for India increased. Incidentally, my Instagram is also flooded with peers fleeing the nation. The disappointment and despair kept compounding for over a week.

Eights chapters of depressing facts later, the book asks some questions. If India is such a hopeless case, then what has still kept it in place ? How has it managed to escape disintegration? Where does it derive its strength from ? The book argues that any society, composed of people, has a character of its own. And an Indian society is a “palimpsest” — a manuscript on which new a later writing has been superimposed. A 5000 year old civilisation, which is surviving and thriving in spite of its systemic flaws, in spite of the corruption and in spite of the gods — the superstition that contributes to the society’s regression.

Despite all this and much more, India has elements that keep it intact. As the book observes, it is a “one percent society”. Ever since independence, it has improved by 1% in any social or economical indicator. Whether, that is its poverty, its literacy levels, or any other HDI Indicator. Given the magnitude of shit it has to clean up, this maybe very small, but this indicates, that India prefers “stability over efficiency”. Osho once said, “Human beings are supposed to be graceful and not efficient” — I think, India is a strong independent woman whose frontal lobes are overtiming to keep it growing albeit slowly.

In 1991, it was on the brink of bankruptcy. India-Pakistan standoff has lurched on a nuclear war several times in the past. Unlike homogenous cultures, one corner of India can be protesting while the other might be celebrating a festival. India is a “functioning anarchy”. It submits its assignment in the last minute of the extended deadline.

This book made me relate with my country and by the end of it, my erstwhile groan had been stretched into a chamkaan-vaali-muskaan. By now, I was contented and convinced, only to be confounded by the last line, when the author says that someone once told him, “Remember, India always wins.” This was cringe and funny. It took me straight to a teary-eyed Rohan from K3G, who, while looking at Rahul rejoicing over India’s victory in a cricket match says, “Jeet jayenge”.

P.S. Why does India sound romantic only when one is not in India ?
