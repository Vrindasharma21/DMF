Drought in Monsoon

This monsoon hasn’t disappointed us in disappointing us and is undoubtedly as miserable as the previous two monsoons. Surprisingly, the misery is unprecedented. It is so that I have been identified by people as an extremist. As history textbooks have it, extremists are stupid people who see only black or white and not one, forget fifty shades of grey. I was a prominent political and emotional extremist. I left political extremism ever since I found that being on either end of that spectrum, going from left to right, leads either to detention or unfunny brainrot. Leaving that however is not the source of my misery because leaving is never as much a misery as being left is. Turns out, emotional extremism has left me, if recent reports from well wishers are to be believed. Let me support my case.

I recently watched Grave of the Fireflies. Past Lives before that. Even before any of the unemployed movie watching business started, I made some should-be-heartbreaking-but-why-are-you-still-smiling goodbyes to my friends and family. The problem is that the earlier me, would have howled through these experiences. I would have writhed on the streets. And slapped myself and anyone around, out of sheer emotional pain. I have always identified with kids who lie down and cry in the middle of a store or with fisherwomen who beat up their chests on the streets and went bald when the ex chief minister of Tamil Nadu, Jayalalitha died at the age of 68 due to a cardiac arrest.

I used to think, thus, that my emotional pendulum oscillates between deliriously happy and dehydrating-ly sad. Now it seems suddenly that we have lost the fucking pendulum. While I am used to losing things, be it my bicycle, bag, phone, ID card, temper or friends, my tears have always come in very handy to me, until not. We are facing a drought like situation in the middle of flooding monsoon.
Earlier as a kid, if I hadn’t cried in a week, I would become uneasy. Whenever these urges to cry came upon me, I would start reminiscing things about the past or hallucinating depressing situations that could induce tears. I had a collection of such mental fabrications, just like people have porn collections. Sometimes, I would try to save a drowning doll kept on the floor, while I hung my hand downwards lying on the table, trying to catch hold of the doll’s hand, who was actually my father in the Atlantic Ocean after the Titanic had sunk. Or I would act as if I were a rickshawallah, pulling my cycle throughout the house and then someone would beat me up, and I would cry at the injustices of life. In one of my personal favourites, I howled through the entire duration of Taare Zameen Par because I imagined my brother to be Ishan Awasthi and I was really sad for whatever was happening to him.

Later as my imagination started sucking because of more maths, I made cry-ology easier for myself. Now, I would re watch “special movie scenes” or listen to some “special songs” to evoke memories. However, such is the cruelty of capitalism that most special songs and movie scenes today have millions of views and they are special to every other person. Today a story with my special song in it, is reposted by twenty thousand people, and can destroy my peace of mind any bloody second. My special movie scene was debunked and analysed like a theory in a podcast, my special rooftop has been permanently closed, my special spot has been replaced by an awful glass building — the sheer amount of change and information one has to deal with is overwhelming and mind numbing, to say the least.

All this feels as if someone is ambushing me. I am in a maze. Every street I walk in, has a blazing hot armed soldier called “blast-from-the-past” ready to attack me. It is a demon who will be destroyed when its fire is extinguished by my tears. But first I need to defend myself from it and thus, I need a shield in the maze. The catch is, tears come only when you let go of the shield.

My solution to my imaginary maze is that someone else should do the shielding for you while you destroy your demons. But, it is not as easy as it sounds because this someone might have a leaky shield, or they might not be able to hold the shield for long enough or worse, they could be a demon in disguise. Maybe a very very good ideal shield will be leakless, enduring and honest that makes one feel safe.
Or maybe, monsoon has driven me on the contours of madness. What else could one even expect from a gloomy, sunless, universal mourning like situation where all everyone ever does is cry…
